Public Safety Blueprint Household, Farm & Urban Defense

How to Prepare, Survive & What NOT To Do

Proactive preparation saves lives, protects hard-earned farm yields, and insulates households from catastrophic climate shocks. Follow this structured 3-phase action roadmap tailored to Nigerian realities.

🌱 Phase 1: Before (Preparation) | ☀️ Phase 2: During (Survival Guide) | 🚫 Phase 3: What NOT To Do | 📋 Interactive Readiness Audit
Phase 1: Proactive Action

Preparation Before El Niño Strikes

Early intervention is 10 times cheaper than emergency disaster relief. Implement these measures months before the peak dry or rainy seasons begin.

💧

1. Water Harvesting & Reserves

Install rainwater collection gutters channeling into clean overhead Geepee/plastic tanks. Keep tanks tightly sealed with insect netting to prevent disease-carrying mosquito breeding and harmattan dust infiltration.

Domestic Safety Covered Reservoirs
🌾

2. Drought-Resilient Agriculture

Transition crops to drought-tolerant, fast-maturing certified seeds: Cowpea, Sorghum, Millet, and Vitamin A Cassava stems from IITA/Ministry of Agriculture. Mulch farmland heavily using dry maize stalks or leaves to lock moisture in the topsoil.

Food Security Certified Hardy Seeds
🧹

3. Unclog Community Drainages

Organize neighborhood sanitation to clear clogged street gutters, culverts, and water channels of plastic bottles and silt. Blocked drainage is the #1 cause of catastrophic urban flash floods in Lagos, Ibadan, Onitsha, and Port Harcourt.

Flood Defense Clean Waterways
🩹

4. Emergency First-Aid Kit

Assemble a dedicated emergency climate kit: packets of Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS), WaterGuard or chlorine tablets, fever antipyretics (paracetamol), battery-powered torchlights, and essential prescription medications.

Health Defense ORS & WaterGuard
🏠

5. Structural & Roof Reinforcement

El Niño thunderstorms are accompanied by ferocious wind gusts. Inspect roofing zinc sheets, replace rusted nails with rubber-washer roofing screws, and prune high tree branches hanging directly above residential quarters.

Shelter Defense Wind-Resistant Roofing
📻

6. Weather Broadcast Monitoring

Keep a battery-powered radio or check official NIMET weather apps regularly. Establish a community WhatsApp or SMS alert chain with your village head, community development association (CDA), or trade union.

Early Warning NIMET Forecasts
Phase 2: Active Survival

Surviving During El Niño Conditions

When extreme heatwaves or unexpected flash floods hit your area, adopting proper immediate survival behavior prevents fatalities and serious illness.

☀️
Heatwave Survival Protocol

Managing Extreme Heat (38°C – 43°C)

  • ✓ Hydrate Proactively: Drink clean water even before feeling thirsty. Aim for at least 3 to 4 liters daily during heat peaks.
  • ✓ Avoid Peak Sun (11:00 AM – 4:00 PM): Reschedule farm tilling and heavy manual labor to early morning (6:00 AM - 9:30 AM) or dusk.
  • ✓ Wear Loose, Light Cotton: Light-colored, breathable fabrics reflect heat. Avoid thick dark synthetic polyester which traps body heat.
  • ✓ Natural Indoor Cooling (Without Grid Light): Open cross-ventilation windows at night; hang damp cotton cloths near open windows to generate natural evaporative cooling.
  • ✓ Protect High-Risk Individuals: Regularly bathe infants and elderly family members with lukewarm (not freezing) water to stabilize body temperature.
💧 Homemade Emergency ORS Recipe (For Dehydration)

Mix 1 Litre of clean boiled/filtered water + 6 level teaspoons of sugar + 1/2 level teaspoon of salt. Stir until dissolved. Administer in small, frequent sips to anyone showing signs of heat exhaustion or diarrhea.

🌊
Torrential Surge Protocol

Surviving Sudden Flash Floods

  • ✓ Move Instantly to Higher Ground: If flood waters enter your home or street, do not wait for water levels to rise above knees. Evacuate immediately.
  • ✓ Kill Electrical Mains: Turn off your building's main circuit breaker immediately before water reaches electrical sockets to avoid lethal shock.
  • ✓ Never Drive Through Flooded Bridges: Just 30cm (1 foot) of moving flood water can sweep away private sedans, tricycles (Keke NAPEP), and motorcycles.
  • ✓ Beware of Hidden Manholes & Reptiles: Submerged drainage slabs, open gutters, and displaced snakes are lethal hazards in brown floodwaters.
  • ✓ Strictly Treat All Ingested Water: Flood water mixes with open septic pits. Boil all cooking and drinking water for at least 10 minutes to stop cholera.
📞 Immediate Trapped Citizen Hotline

If marooned on a rooftop or elevated structure, dial 112 or your state SEMA number immediately. Wave brightly colored clothing or shine a phone flashlight to guide rescue boats.

Phase 3: Vital Warnings

What NOT To Do During El Niño

Human error and dangerous local customs multiply climate tragedies. Avoid these 6 fatal mistakes that have historically cost Nigerian lives and communities.

🔥

1. DO NOT Burn Bush or Farm Refuse

During prolonged El Niño heatwaves, grass and trees turn dry as tinder. A simple trash or agricultural fire carried by sudden dry winds creates raging, uncontainable bushfires that incinerate entire villages, livestock barns, and mature yam barns.

🚯

2. DO NOT Dump Refuse into Gutters

Using rainstorms as a cheap opportunity to empty domestic waste and plastic bags into gutters blocks municipal culverts. This directly causes catastrophic back-flow floods that submerge downstream homes and market stalls.

🚼

3. DO NOT Lock Children Inside Parked Cars

Even for "just five minutes" while entering a market or pharmacy! Under the blistering Nigerian sun, the cabin temperature of a closed car rises past 55°C (131°F) within minutes, causing rapid asphyxiation and fatal heat stroke.

🚱

4. DO NOT Drink Untreated Well Water

Dropping water tables and erratic flash floods cause septic tanks to leach directly into shallow wells and municipal conduits. Never assume clear water is safe—always boil or treat with WaterGuard to avoid lethal cholera and typhoid outbreaks.

🌾

5. DO NOT Plant with the Very First Rain

El Niño creates deceptive "false onsets" of rain. Sowing valuable seeds with the first isolated April or May shower often leads to total crop loss when a 4-week dry scorching spell immediately follows. Always confirm with NIMET agro-bulletins.

⚡

6. DO NOT Approach Fallen Power Lines

Intense El Niño thunderstorm squalls frequently snap high-tension electric cables and overturn transformers into standing puddle water. Never walk near downed electrical poles; report them immediately to DisCos or Fire Service.
